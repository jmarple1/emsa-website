Prospective members

Who can join?

Any Miami student. There are no prerequisites and no dues.

Do I need medical experience?

No. Training is provided.

How much time does it take?

About 1 hour a week.

What does a semester look like?

4 general body meetings, 3 CPR classes, 1 Narcan distribution night, and 1 K–6 Outreach event, plus optional Outreach Team work if you want more.

How do I join, and what happens next?

Fill out the interest form on the Join page. You'll get a confirmation with the GroupMe link; then come to the next meeting.

I'm already an EMT. Is there a role for me?

Yes. EMTs help teach and staff classes and events. EMSA is not a first response unit, but we aspire to build one at Miami, and EMTs who want to help shape that effort are especially welcome.

Class participants

Which courses do you offer?

AHA BLS, Heartsaver CPR/First Aid, and Stop the Bleed.

Do I get a real AHA card?

Yes. BLS and Heartsaver participants receive an AHA eCard issued through the Therapeutic Professionals Training Center.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Classes are free.

How long is my card valid?

Two years, through the end of the month it was issued.

When and where are classes?

On campus, usually on Sundays. Dates are on the Events calendar.

How do I register?

Through the registration form on the CPR Classes page.

Partners

Who leads EMSA, and who is the faculty advisor?

Co-Presidents Max Ilecki and Jordan Vandeventer lead EMSA, and Leslie Haxby-McNeill is the faculty advisor. Additional officers will be listed after elections.

What are your affiliations and recognition?

Max and Jordan are certified AHA instructors, and EMSA runs an AHA Heart Club. EMSA distributes naloxone through Project DAWN (Ohio Department of Health) and runs its fraternity-house harm reduction program with the Butler County General Health District and Miami's Office of Student Wellness. EMSA is a National Collegiate EMS Foundation (NCEMSF) member organization, received NCEMSF's New Group Initiative grant, and was named Miami University's 2024–25 New Student Organization of the Year.

How do you distribute naloxone and test strips in line with Ohio requirements?

EMSA distributes naloxone through Ohio's Project DAWN program, and its fraternity-house program runs with the Butler County General Health District and Miami's Office of Student Wellness. Kits are free and may not be resold.

Does EMSA respond to emergencies?

No. EMSA is not a first response unit; in an emergency, call 911. Building a student EMT first response unit is a long-term goal.
