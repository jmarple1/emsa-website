Call 911.

EMS Alliance does not respond to emergencies. If someone is seriously hurt or ill, call 911 right away.

Tell the dispatcher

  • Where you are: the address or building, floor, and room.
  • What happened.
  • Whether the person is awake and breathing.

Stay on the line and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Don't hang up until they tell you to.

Until help arrives

  • Stay with the person.
  • Send someone to bring the nearest AED.
  • Send someone to meet the responders and show them the way.

Hands-only CPR

For a teen or adult who suddenly collapses, doesn't respond, and isn't breathing normally (or is only gasping):

  1. Call 911, or have someone call, and send someone for an AED.
  2. Push hard and fast in the center of the chest: at least 2 inches deep, 100 to 120 pushes a minute. Let the chest come back up between pushes.
  3. Keep going until an AED is ready to use, trained help takes over, or the person starts breathing normally.

Using an AED

  1. Turn the AED on and follow its voice prompts.
  2. Attach the pads to the person's bare chest, placed as the pictures on the pads show.
  3. Make sure no one is touching the person while the AED checks the heart rhythm or delivers a shock.
  4. After a shock, or if no shock is advised, start CPR again right away and keep following the prompts.

Suspected opioid overdose

  1. Call 911.
  2. If the person isn't breathing normally, start CPR right away and keep going. If you're trained, give breaths with compressions; if not, hands-only CPR still helps.
  3. Give naloxone (Narcan) as soon as you can, following the directions on the package. Naloxone doesn't replace CPR. If there's no response after 2 to 3 minutes, give another dose if you have one.
  4. If the person is breathing, roll them onto their side and stay with them until help arrives.
